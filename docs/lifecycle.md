# Product Lifecycle

The lifecycle defines how product work progresses from business need to shipped outcome.

## Stages

| Stage | Purpose | Exit Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intake | Capture demand and source context. | Request is logged, PM owner assigned, initial business sponsor identified. |
| Discovery | Understand the problem and business outcome. | Problem, users, goals, constraints, success measures, and options are documented. |
| Definition | Convert discovery into a buildable product brief. | Scope, requirements, non-goals, acceptance criteria, dependencies, and risks are clear. |
| Planning | Align delivery path and communication plan. | Owners, milestones, capacity, dependencies, and launch approach are confirmed. |
| Build | Execute with controlled scope and visible progress. | Work meets acceptance criteria and is ready for validation. |
| Validation | Confirm product, customer, operational, and launch readiness. | Quality, support, GTM, and stakeholder readiness are confirmed. |
| Launch | Release the product or change to intended users. | Launch is communicated and adoption or usage tracking begins. |
| Post-Launch | Measure outcome and capture learning. | Outcomes, issues, lessons, and follow-up actions are documented. |
| Lifecycle Management | Maintain, improve, reposition, sunset, or retire. | Current ownership and next lifecycle decision are clear. |

## Stage Gate Rule

Work may move forward only when the exit standard for the current stage is met or an accountable leader explicitly accepts the risk of moving forward early.

## Definition of Ready

Work is ready to build when:

- The customer or business problem is clear.
- The target users or impacted groups are known.
- Success criteria are defined.
- Requirements and non-goals are documented.
- Acceptance criteria are testable.
- Dependencies and risks are visible.
- Product and Engineering agree the work is sufficiently defined.
- The communication plan is appropriate for the size and impact of the work.
