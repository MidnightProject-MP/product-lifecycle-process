# PM Operating Principles

These principles describe the PM behaviors the lifecycle process should embed by default.

## 1. Proactive Visibility

PMs are responsible for making status, risks, decisions, and next steps visible before stakeholders have to ask.

Default behaviors:

- Publish regular initiative updates.
- Call out risks early.
- Maintain a clear decision log.
- Name what changed since the last update.
- Make the next milestone obvious.

Process requirements:

- Every active initiative has a current status update.
- Stage changes are communicated to affected stakeholders.
- Risks and blockers are named with an owner and next action.

## 2. Business-Led Requirements Discovery

PMs actively extract, clarify, and pressure-test business needs instead of waiting for complete specs.

Default behaviors:

- Interview business stakeholders.
- Clarify the problem before solutioning.
- Ask what outcome matters and how success will be measured.
- Identify edge cases, constraints, customer impact, and operational needs.
- Translate business intent into product requirements.

Process requirements:

- Discovery cannot close until the problem, users, goals, constraints, and success measures are documented.
- Intake requests are converted from asks into problem statements.
- PM owns requirement completeness before build readiness.

## 3. Build Readiness Discipline

PMs protect engineering focus and delivery quality by holding the line when work is not ready.

Default behaviors:

- Push back on vague asks.
- Refuse to move work into build without acceptance criteria.
- Surface tradeoffs clearly.
- Separate urgent from underdefined.
- Escalate constructively when pressure bypasses the process.

Process requirements:

- Work cannot move into build without meeting the Definition of Ready.
- "Not ready" is a valid status when the work needs more discovery, definition, or alignment.
- Business overrides must be explicit and visible.

## 4. Cross-Functional Trust

PMs create trust by being transparent, prepared, consistent, and respectful of each function's expertise.

Default behaviors:

- Bring Engineering, Design, and business partners in early.
- Share context, not just tasks.
- Represent business goals accurately.
- Avoid surprise scope changes.
- Credit contributors and close the loop.

Process requirements:

- Cross-functional review occurs before build commitment.
- Product, Engineering, Design, Business, and GTM owners are named when relevant.
- Risks, dependencies, and decisions are visible to the team.

## 5. Ownership and Managed Escalation

PMs own the product problem end to end, even when they do not own every solution or dependency.

Default behaviors:

- Drive ambiguity toward clarity.
- Manage up when blocked.
- Ask for help early.
- Own the communication path.
- Grow into unfamiliar domains instead of deflecting.

Process requirements:

- Every initiative has one accountable PM.
- Every blocker has an owner, escalation path, and target resolution date.
- Post-launch review captures outcomes, lessons, and follow-up ownership.
